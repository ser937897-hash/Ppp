# Editorial standards: English

Anchors: The Chicago Manual of Style (books, long-form; default), AP Stylebook (news, web, marketing), Merriam-Webster (spelling, US) / Oxford (UK); Strunk & White *The Elements of Style*; William Zinsser *On Writing Well*; Orwell's six rules ("Politics and the English Language"); Steven Pinker *The Sense of Style*; Renni Browne & Dave King *Self-Editing for Fiction Writers*; Benjamin Dreyer *Dreyer's English*.

Pick CMOS or AP in the brief and never mix them. Pick US or UK spelling and stay with it.

## Levels of editing

1. **Developmental / structural** — concept, argument or plot, structure, pacing, character, audience fit. Output: editorial letter + margin notes.
2. **Line editing** — sentence and paragraph craft: clarity, rhythm, voice, word choice, tension, transitions.
3. **Copyediting** (light / medium / heavy) — grammar, usage, consistency, style-guide conformance, style sheet, fact flags.
4. **Proofreading** — final cold read of near-final copy: only incontestable errors, layout glitches, missing words.

Terminology is not standardized across houses — confirm scope with the client.

## Prose rules

- Orwell: cut every word you can; prefer the short word; avoid stale metaphors; active voice where the actor matters; plain English over jargon; break any rule sooner than say anything barbarous.
- Kill nominalizations ("make a decision" → "decide"), hedges ("somewhat", "arguably" stacked), intensifiers ("very", "really", "incredibly").
- Concrete over abstract; specific numbers over adjectives.
- Vary sentence length; put the stress at the end of the sentence.
- Fiction: show emotion through action, subtext and sensory detail; limit dialogue tags to "said/asked"; no head-hopping within a scene; avoid filter words ("she saw", "he felt", "noticed").

## Mechanics checklist

- Serial (Oxford) comma: yes in CMOS, no in AP.
- Em dash — closed in CMOS ("word—word"), spaced in AP ("word — word"); en dash for ranges (1990–1995).
- Curly quotes; US: periods and commas inside quotes; UK: logical punctuation, single quotes.
- Numbers: CMOS spells out zero–one hundred; AP spells out one–nine.
- Headline case: CMOS title case; many web outlets use sentence case — follow the brief.
- Common usage traps: affect/effect, compose/comprise, fewer/less, that/which (US), lay/lie, who/whom, its/it's, dangling modifiers.
